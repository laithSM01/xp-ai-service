# Gym Injuries Knowledge Base

---

## Knee Pain

**Aliases:** knee soreness, knee discomfort, patellofemoral pain, runner's knee, jumper's knee, patellar tendinopathy, chondromalacia

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Deep squats (below 90 degrees / past parallel)
- Full-range leg extensions (knee extension machine)
- High-impact plyometrics (box jumps, jump squats, jump lunges)
- Running on hard surfaces or long-distance running
- Heavy barbell lunges with full knee bend
- Leg press with heavy weight through full range
- Stair sprints

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Partial-range squats (45–60 degrees only, bodyweight or light load)
- Seated and lying leg curls
- Hip abductor/adductor machine
- Low-resistance cycling or stationary bike
- Swimming (flutter kick only, avoid breaststroke kick)
- Wall sits (limited range)
- Step-ups on a low box (15–20 cm)
- Seated calf raises
- Hip bridges and glute bridges
- Upper body pressing and pulling movements

**Technique Notes:**
- Knee must track directly over the second toe — no inward collapse (valgus)
- Avoid locking out the knee under any load
- Reduce range of motion before reducing load
- Strengthen glutes and hip abductors to offload the knee joint
- Avoid any exercise that causes pain during or after; pain = stop
- Ice for 15–20 minutes after training if swelling is present

## ACL Injury/Recovery

**Aliases:** anterior cruciate ligament tear, ACL tear, ACL reconstruction, post-ACL surgery, ACL rehab, ACL sprain

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Any cutting, pivoting, or lateral change-of-direction movements
- Single-leg landing from height
- Jump squats, box jumps, depth jumps
- Full-speed sprinting until cleared by physio
- Leg extensions (full range, resisted)
- Contact sports and agility drills until minimum 9 months post-surgery
- Deep squats with heavy load in early/mid recovery

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Straight-leg raises
- Quad sets (isometric quadriceps)
- Seated hamstring curls (light resistance)
- Hip bridges and single-leg hip bridges (later stages)
- Terminal knee extensions (TKE) with resistance band
- Low-resistance stationary cycling
- Swimming (no breaststroke kick)
- Calf raises (seated, then standing)
- Upper body exercises (pressing, pulling, rowing)
- Leg press through safe partial range (phase-dependent)

**Technique Notes:**
- Recovery is phase-based: always treat as early rehab unless client states post-surgery timeline and clearance
- Avoid any knee valgus stress
- Hamstring and hip strengthening are critical to protect the graft
- Never program single-leg plyometrics or pivoting without explicit physio clearance
- Prioritize neuromuscular control before adding load
- "Post-ACL surgery" defaults to conservative program regardless of stated timeline

## Lower Back Pain

**Aliases:** lumbar pain, back pain, lumbar strain, lumbar ache, low back pain, lumbago, sacral pain, lower back discomfort

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Heavy barbell deadlifts (conventional and sumo) in acute phase
- Heavy barbell squats with forward lean
- Good mornings
- Sit-ups and full crunches with hip flexor involvement
- Leg raises with unsupported lower back
- Hyperextensions / back extensions with heavy load
- Jefferson curls
- Heavy barbell rows with poor bracing
- Twisting movements under load (cable woodchops with heavy weight)

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Dead bugs
- Bird dogs
- Cat-cow stretch
- Glute bridges and hip thrusts (bodyweight to light load)
- Pallof press (anti-rotation core)
- Suitcase carries (light, unilateral)
- Lat pulldown and seated cable row (neutral spine maintained)
- Leg press (moderate range, no posterior pelvic tilt at bottom)
- Plank variations (no sagging hips)
- Reverse hypers (light, if available)

**Technique Notes:**
- Brace the core (360-degree intra-abdominal pressure) before every loaded movement
- Neutral spine is mandatory — avoid both excessive arch and rounding
- Avoid exercises that cause pain, numbness, or tingling radiating into the leg (may indicate disc involvement)
- Strengthen posterior chain (glutes, hamstrings) to reduce lumbar load
- Hip hinge pattern must be mastered before adding load

## Shoulder Impingement

**Aliases:** rotator cuff impingement, swimmer's shoulder, shoulder bursitis, subacromial impingement, shoulder pinching, shoulder clicking with pain

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Overhead pressing above 90 degrees with internal rotation (behind-the-neck press, upright rows)
- Upright rows (especially with close grip)
- Dumbbell/barbell lateral raises above shoulder height
- Full-range overhead shoulder press (barbell, especially behind-neck)
- Pull-ups/chin-ups with wide, fully locked-out overhead grip if painful
- Bench press with wide grip causing shoulder pain
- Any overhead throw or ballistic shoulder movement

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Cable face pulls (external rotation emphasis)
- Band external rotations
- Dumbbell lateral raises to 80 degrees only (not full overhead)
- Seated row (neutral grip, retract scapula)
- Neutral-grip lat pulldown (not wide grip behind neck)
- Low-incline dumbbell press (30–45 degrees, neutral grip)
- Rear delt flies
- Scapular wall slides
- Shrugs (controlled)

**Technique Notes:**
- Keep shoulder blades retracted and depressed during all pressing movements
- Avoid internal rotation at or above shoulder height
- Never let shoulders roll forward under load
- Strengthen external rotators (infraspinatus, teres minor) in every session
- Cable face pulls should be included in every upper-body session
- If overhead pressing is included, limit to pain-free range only and use neutral/hammer grip dumbbells

## Rotator Cuff Injury

**Aliases:** rotator cuff tear, rotator cuff strain, SITS muscles injury, rotator cuff tendinitis, rotator cuff tendinopathy, partial rotator cuff tear

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Overhead pressing of any kind in acute or partial tear stage
- Behind-the-neck press and pull
- Upright rows
- Any movement causing shoulder pain above 60 degrees abduction
- Bench press with wide flare at high weight
- Dumbbell flyes with heavy weight (severe stretch on inferior cuff)
- Throwing and ballistic shoulder movements

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Isometric shoulder exercises (early recovery: wall pressing isometrics)
- Pendulum swings (passive range)
- Band external rotations (side-lying or standing)
- Cable internal and external rotation (low pulley)
- Scapular stabilization: prone Y, T, W exercises
- Seated row (neutral grip, light)
- Low-load elbow flexion (bicep curls, not involving shoulder)
- Lower body training (legs, hip-dominant)

**Technique Notes:**
- Partial tears require medical clearance before any loaded shoulder work
- All shoulder work must be pain-free throughout range
- Never load through pain — stop immediately if sharp pain occurs
- Prioritize scapular stabilizer strength before rotator cuff direct work
- Return to overhead pressing only after full pain-free passive range restored and rotator cuff strength symmetrical

## Tennis Elbow

**Aliases:** lateral epicondylitis, lateral elbow pain, lateral epicondylopathy, outer elbow pain, golfer's elbow (medial variant)

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Barbell wrist curls and wrist extensions
- Reverse barbell curls (heavy)
- Barbell rows with overhand grip at high volume
- Dumbbell lateral raises with tight grip
- Any exercise causing pain along the lateral (outer) elbow
- Tight-grip pulling (chin-ups, lat pulldown) in acute phase

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Tyler twist (eccentric wrist extension with therabar/rubber bar)
- Eccentric wrist extension with light dumbbell (over table edge)
- Pronation/supination with hammer or light dumbbell
- Neutral-grip lat pulldown
- Neutral-grip cable row
- Tricep pushdown (rope attachment, pain-free range)
- Lower body dominant training
- Forearm stretching: wrist flexion and extension stretches

**Technique Notes:**
- Eccentric loading is the primary therapeutic approach for tendinopathy
- Avoid gripping hard; use thicker bar attachments (fat grips) to reduce tendon stress
- Neutral grip preferred over pronated grip for all pulling movements
- Apply a counterforce brace (epicondyle strap) during training if recommended
- Reduce total pulling volume until pain settles

## Wrist Pain

**Aliases:** wrist strain, carpal tunnel syndrome, wrist tendinitis, wrist sprain, wrist discomfort, wrist injury, De Quervain's tenosynovitis

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Barbell back squat if wrist extension is painful under load
- Heavy barbell bench press with wrist in hyperextension
- Push-ups on palms if painful (can substitute with fists or push-up handles)
- Barbell wrist curls and extensions
- Handstands and gymnastic wrist loading
- Olympic lifting and front rack position if painful

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Neutral-grip dumbbell press (hammer grip)
- Dumbbell rows (neutral grip)
- Cable exercises with rope or neutral attachments
- Machine-based pressing and pulling
- Lower body training (squats with goblet variation using handles if needed)
- Forearm and wrist mobility: tendon glides, wrist circles
- Reverse wrist curls (light, pain-free range for rehabilitation)

**Technique Notes:**
- Maintain neutral wrist alignment throughout pressing movements — no hyperextension
- Wrist wraps can be used to support during heavier loads
- Reduce grip-intensive work; use straps for pulling exercises
- Prioritize wrist mobility and finger tendon glide exercises
- Any tingling, numbness, or burning in the fingers requires medical assessment (possible nerve involvement)

## Hip Flexor Strain

**Aliases:** hip flexor pull, iliopsoas strain, iliopsoas injury, groin pull (proximal), hip flexor tightness with pain, anterior hip pain

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Running and sprinting in acute phase
- High knees and dynamic hip flexion under load
- Leg raises and hanging knee raises (high hip flexion demand)
- Squat variations with heavy forward lean
- Seated leg press with knees driven deep toward chest
- Cycling at high resistance (hip flexion-dominant)
- Straight-leg deadlift if causing anterior hip pain

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Hip flexor stretching (kneeling lunge stretch, Thomas stretch position)
- Glute bridges and hip thrusts
- Clamshells and lateral band walks (hip external rotation)
- Leg curls (seated or lying)
- Calf raises
- Upper body pressing and pulling
- Swimming (gentle flutter kick)
- Quad stretch and hip flexor mobilization

**Technique Notes:**
- Rest from hip-flexion-dominant activity until acute pain resolves
- Strengthen hip extensors (glutes, hamstrings) to reduce iliopsoas overload
- Progressive return to hip-flexion loading through pain-free range
- Address anterior pelvic tilt which chronically over-activates hip flexors

## Hamstring Strain

**Aliases:** hamstring pull, hamstring tear, hamstring injury, posterior thigh strain, biceps femoris strain, hamstring tendinopathy

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Sprinting and running in acute phase
- Straight-leg deadlifts and Romanian deadlifts with heavy load in acute phase
- Leg curls through full range under heavy load in acute phase
- Explosive hip extension (kettlebell swings, snatches) in early recovery
- Any movement producing pain at the proximal (upper) hamstring attachment
- Nordic curls in acute/early recovery

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Isometric hamstring holds (early phase: prone leg holds at various angles)
- Gentle prone knee flexion (early mobility)
- Glute bridges (bilateral, then single-leg as tolerated)
- Hip thrusts (bilateral)
- Leg press (focus on quad-dominant, limited knee bend)
- Upper body pulling and pressing
- Swimming (no flutter kick in acute phase)
- Eccentric loading program (Nordic curls, slow RDL) — late-stage recovery only

**Technique Notes:**
- Graded return: isometric → isotonic light → eccentric → explosive
- Proximal hamstring tears (near sit bone) require longer recovery and conservative loading
- Pain during or after exercise = reduce load and range immediately
- Stretching acutely strained hamstrings can worsen the injury — avoid aggressive static stretch in first 2 weeks
- Return to sprinting only after full pain-free strength symmetry is restored

## IT Band Syndrome

**Aliases:** ITBS, iliotibial band syndrome, lateral knee pain from running, IT band friction syndrome, lateral hip/knee tightness with pain

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Running (especially downhill) in acute phase
- Leg press with extreme foot placement variation
- Side-lying hip abduction with heavy weight (can aggravate)
- Any repetitive knee flexion/extension under load causing lateral knee pain
- Deep squats if causing lateral knee pain

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Hip abductor strengthening: clamshells, lateral band walks
- Single-leg glute bridge
- Hip hinge movements (RDL, kettlebell swing) — controlled
- IT band stretching: cross-leg standing stretch, supine IT band stretch
- Foam rolling: lateral quad, TFL, glutes (not directly on IT band attachment at knee)
- Swimming
- Cycling (only if pain-free, ensure proper saddle height)
- Step-ups (pain-free)

**Technique Notes:**
- ITBS is overuse/friction — address training load and hip weakness, not just the knee
- Strengthen hip abductors (glute medius) — weakness is a primary driver
- Check for foot overpronation and poor running mechanics if relevant
- Foam rolling the lateral thigh can reduce tissue tension but is not a substitute for hip strengthening
- Gradual return to running only after hip strengthening program is established

## Ankle Sprain

**Aliases:** rolled ankle, twisted ankle, lateral ankle sprain, inversion ankle sprain, ankle ligament injury

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Running and jumping in acute phase
- Single-leg balance work on unstable surface in acute phase
- Calf raises if ankle is too painful or swollen (acute phase)
- Box jumps and plyometrics
- Any lateral cutting or pivoting movement
- Heavy barbell squat if ankle dorsiflexion is restricted or painful

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Seated ankle alphabet (early mobility)
- Ankle pumps (dorsiflexion/plantarflexion) — early phase
- Theraband ankle inversion/eversion (gentle resistance — later phase)
- Single-leg balance on flat surface (once swelling resolves)
- Calf raises (bilateral first, progress to single-leg)
- Hip and core work (lying or seated)
- Upper body training
- Cycling (seated, low resistance)

**Technique Notes:**
- RICE protocol in first 48–72 hours: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation
- Maintain range of motion early; immobility leads to stiffness
- Proprioception and balance training is critical to prevent reinjury
- Return to running only after full pain-free ROM and equal single-leg balance restored
- High-grade (Grade III) sprains may require surgical consultation

## Neck Pain

**Aliases:** cervical pain, neck strain, cervicalgia, whiplash, cervical disc pain, neck stiffness, upper trap pain with neck involvement

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Behind-the-neck press and behind-the-neck lat pulldown
- Shrugs with heavy load causing neck compression
- Barbell back squat with high bar position if cervical discomfort
- Neck harness exercises in acute phase
- Any exercise requiring forced neck flexion or extension under load
- Headbangers / decline sit-ups with neck strain

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Isometric neck exercises (gently resist with hand in all four directions — cervical stabilization)
- Chin tucks (cervical retraction)
- Scapular retraction exercises (rows, face pulls — reduce upper trap tension)
- Cable rows (neutral spine, no neck strain)
- Machine-based chest and lat work (avoids neck loading)
- Lower body work (leg press, leg curl, calf raises)
- Core exercises that do not load the cervical spine

**Technique Notes:**
- Ensure neutral head position throughout all exercises — no forward head posture under load
- Strengthen deep cervical flexors and scapular stabilizers to reduce neck muscle overload
- Tingling, numbness, or pain radiating into the arm/hand requires medical assessment (cervical radiculopathy)
- Avoid any exercise that reproduces or worsens neck pain — stop immediately
- Pillow and sleeping position advice may be relevant if pain is daily

## Chest/Rib Injury

**Aliases:** rib injury, costochondritis, rib fracture, chest wall pain, intercostal strain, rib stress fracture, sternum pain, chest contusion

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- All pressing movements (bench press, push-ups, dips) in acute phase
- Dumbbell flyes and cable flyes (stretch under load)
- Deadlifts and barbell rows (trunk bracing pressure on ribs)
- Heavy overhead press
- Core exercises involving trunk rotation with force (cable woodchops)
- Any exercise requiring deep breath-hold and Valsalva maneuver under high load

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Lower body isolation (leg press, leg curl, leg extension — pain-free range)
- Seated machine exercises (minimal chest involvement)
- Light cable work in pain-free plane
- Walking (pain-free, light aerobic)
- Breathing exercises (slow diaphragmatic breathing for rib mobility restoration)
- Scapular and shoulder mobility work that does not stress the chest wall

**Technique Notes:**
- Rib fractures require complete rest from compression and impact for 4–6 weeks minimum
- Costochondritis (cartilage inflammation): avoid chest compression and deep twisting
- Return to pressing movements gradually, starting with machine press at very light load
- Pain with breathing at rest or severe pain with any movement = immediate medical clearance required
- Monitor for referred pain or tightness that could indicate cardiac causes (refer to GP if uncertain)

## Herniated Disc

**Aliases:** slipped disc, prolapsed disc, disc herniation, bulging disc, disc protrusion, sciatica (disc-related), cervical disc herniation, lumbar disc herniation

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Heavy barbell deadlifts (conventional, sumo, Romanian) in acute phase
- Heavy barbell squats with any spinal flexion
- Sit-ups and crunches (repeated spinal flexion)
- Leg raises with unsupported lumbar spine
- Good mornings
- Loaded spinal rotation (cable woodchops, Russian twists with weight)
- Any exercise causing pain, numbness, or tingling in the leg/arm (radiating pain = nerve involvement)
- Jefferson curls and any end-range spinal flexion under load

**Preferred Exercises:**
- McKenzie extension exercises (press-ups/cobra — for posterior disc herniation)
- Bird dog (spine neutral, anti-flexion/rotation)
- Dead bug
- Glute bridges (unloaded, then progressed)
- Pallof press (anti-rotation)
- Walking (neutral spine)
- Swimming (freestyle, no breaststroke if lumbar pain worsens)
- Leg press (neutral spine, moderate range)
- Seated cable row with neutral spine

**Technique Notes:**
- Disc herniation requires phase-based rehabilitation — acute phase is very conservative
- Avoid any exercise that centralizes or peripheralizes nerve pain (leg/arm symptoms)
- Spinal flexion is typically contraindicated — bias toward extension (varies by disc direction)
- Neutral spine must be maintained under all loaded movements
- Nerve pain (sciatica, arm tingling) requires medical clearance before gym programming
- Never prescribe heavy compound lifts without physio clearance

## Post-Surgery General Recovery

**Aliases:** post-op recovery, post-operative, surgical recovery, post-surgery, general rehabilitation, recovery from operation, post-procedure recovery

**Exercises to Avoid Completely:**
- Any exercise targeting or loading the surgical site directly
- Heavy compound lifts until medical clearance is given
- High-impact activities (running, jumping) without clearance
- Exercises requiring Valsalva or breath-holding at surgical area
- Resistance training above the cleared threshold without medical sign-off
- Contact sports

**Preferred Exercises:**
- Exercises targeting body parts completely remote from the surgical site
- Low-intensity walking (if lower body surgery is not the site)
- Range-of-motion exercises as guided by surgeon/physio
- Isometric exercises at the surgical site (very late phase, with clearance)
- Upper body work if lower body surgery and vice versa
- Swimming after wound closure (with medical clearance)
- Breathing exercises and general mobility

**Technique Notes:**
- Always follow the treating surgeon's and physiotherapist's specific guidelines — they override all defaults
- Programming must be tailored to the specific surgery type and post-operative phase
- Never progress load at the surgical site faster than the medical team advises
- Monitor for signs of infection (redness, warmth, swelling, fever) — stop training and refer immediately
- Mental health support may be relevant for prolonged recovery periods
- Nutrition (protein, vitamin C, zinc) is important for wound healing — note in trainer comments if relevant
